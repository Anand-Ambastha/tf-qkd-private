Interpretation:
- 'forced' rows show what each single model reports even past its own validity
  boundary for that Cn2 -- included to show how much a misapplied model over/
  understates range, not as a recommended operating mode.
- AUTO is the physically appropriate estimate at every distance for a given site's
  Cn2, switching models at the regime boundaries listed above.
- Note that turbulence-aware models can show a LONGER max range than the
  deterministic-only prediction (e.g. weak Cn2: 84-86 km turbulent vs. 79.45 km
  deterministic). This is not an error: the deterministic key-rate formula has a
  hard cutoff (R=0 exactly below a critical transmittance), but under fading the
  occasional upward excursion above that threshold still contributes positively to
  the ergodic average even when the MEAN transmittance has already fallen below
  the deterministic cutoff -- so fading can rescue a small amount of extra range
  right at the edge of outage, on top of the (usually dominant) ergodic-rate
  PENALTY it imposes at shorter, non-marginal distances.
- Conversely, a model applied outside its own validity regime can UNDERSTATE range
  (e.g. moderate Cn2: log-normal forced past sigma_R^2=1 gives 72.73 km vs. the
  correctly-applicable Gamma-Gamma/corrected/AUTO value of 79.45 km) -- log-normal's
  known underestimate of tail heaviness outside its weak-turbulence regime cuts
  both ways depending on which tail matters for the quantity being computed.
